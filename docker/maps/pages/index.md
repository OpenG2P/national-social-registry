  1. NOTHING may be called `name` — not a SQL alias, not a GeoJSON property.

## Regions

```sql l1_head
-- The selected top-level area, each metric expressed as a delta against the national
-- figure. A coverage number means little on its own; "6 points below national"
-- is what tells an administrator where to look.
with nat as (
  select round(avg(pct_enrolled), 1)        as n_enrolled,
         round(avg(pct_no_id), 1)           as n_no_id,
         round(avg(pct_poorest_covered), 1) as n_poorest
  from nsr.level1_summary
)
select r.*,
       round(r.pct_enrolled        - nat.n_enrolled, 1) as d_enrolled,
       round(r.pct_no_id           - nat.n_no_id, 1)    as d_no_id,
       round(r.pct_poorest_covered - nat.n_poorest, 1)  as d_poorest
from nsr.level1_summary r cross join nat
where r.pcode = '${inputs.sel_l1.pcode}'
```

```sql l2_head
-- Same metrics again, now compared against the PARENT REGION rather than
-- national — the relevant benchmark once you are inside a parent area.
with parent as (
  select pct_enrolled as p_enrolled,
         pct_no_id    as p_no_id,
         pct_poorest_covered as p_poorest
  from nsr.level1_summary
  where pcode = '${inputs.sel_l1.pcode}'
)
select z.*,
       round(z.pct_enrolled        - parent.p_enrolled, 1) as d_enrolled,
       round(z.pct_no_id           - parent.p_no_id, 1)    as d_no_id,
       round(z.pct_poorest_covered - parent.p_poorest, 1)  as d_poorest
from nsr.level2_summary z cross join parent
where z.pcode = '${inputs.sel_l2.pcode}'
```

```sql l3_in_l2
-- Counts, not suppressed rates. A percentage over ~11 households moves ~9 points
-- per household, so the old columns were blanked below 30 households and the
-- table was mostly empty. The same facts as counts are exact at any size, need
-- no suppression, and are what an administrator can act on.
--
-- Ordered by the size of the enrolment gap, so the top row is where to go first
-- rather than merely where the poverty score is highest — those are not the same

    area_name,
    households,
    poverty_score,
    poorest_not_enrolled,
    enrolled_hh,
    no_id_hh,
    pcode
from nsr.level3_summary
where parent_pcode = '${inputs.sel_l2.pcode}'
order by poorest_not_enrolled desc, poverty_score desc
```

## Woredas {#if l2_head.length}in {l2_head[0].area_name}{/if}— where to act

High poverty with low enrolment means eligible households are being missed. A
high no-ID share means they cannot be paid even once enrolled — a different
problem needing a different fix.

The charts below plot poverty score and household counts rather than coverage,
because coverage is suppressed under 30 households and at this sample scale only
50 of 1,135 areas clear that bar — a coverage chart here would be almost
entirely empty. The table keeps the percentages, blank where they cannot be
trusted, with the household count beside them so a blank is self-explanatory.
Re-seeding at a larger scale, or using a pack with fewer leaf units, fills them
in.

```sql l3_head
-- The clicked leaf area. Only measures that survive at this scale are shown:

-- 50 of the 1,135 areas in this sample.
```

### Which barrier? — all regions

Coverage of the poorest against the share of households with no foundational ID.
A region low on coverage **and** high on no-ID has an identity problem — people
cannot be paid even once enrolled. Low coverage with low no-ID is a targeting and
outreach problem instead. The two need different fixes, which is why they are
plotted against each other rather than ranked separately.
